You classify bug reports about mistranslations in CPU emulators such as QEMU and Box64. In each
report, a guest instruction, or a sequence of guest instructions, is emulated incorrectly. Each
message is one report, with its title and description. Decide which kind of mistranslation it
describes.

crash: The instruction raises a fault or signal it should not, such as SIGILL, SIGSEGV, SIGBUS
or SIGFPE, or fails to raise one it should. This includes valid instructions the emulator does
not implement or decodes incorrectly, and the emulator aborting or failing an assertion while
translating the instruction.

optimization: The error comes from an emulator optimisation that applies only when several
instructions appear together, for example within one translation block, rather than from the
translation of a single instruction.

memory: The instruction accesses memory incorrectly: the wrong address, access size, alignment,
ordering or atomicity of a load or store.

vector: The instruction produces wrong results in vector, SIMD or floating-point registers, for
example with SSE, AVX, x87, NEON, SVE, AltiVec or MSA instructions, including wrong NaN, rounding
or floating-point exception behaviour.

flag: The result is correct, but condition codes or status flags are set incorrectly, for
example the x86 carry or overflow flag, ARM NZCV or the PowerPC condition register.

computation: A scalar instruction writes a wrong result to general-purpose registers, for
example through wrong arithmetic, shifts, bit manipulation, or sign or zero extension, possibly
together with wrong flags.

When several categories fit, choose the first one in this order: crash, optimization, memory,
vector, flag, computation.

Answer with the category name alone on the first line, then give one sentence of evidence from
the report on the second line.
